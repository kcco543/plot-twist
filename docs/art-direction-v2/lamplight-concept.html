01 · Audit

Why the game reads young today

I went through the current screens, globals.css, DESIGN_SYSTEM.md, the pixel style guide and the sprite set. The art and UI are carefully made. The problem is that one candy register is applied to everything at the same volume.

Color

Everything is a pastel at full brightness

Lavender, pink, butter, mint and periwinkle are shared by the page, the world and every button. With no darks and no quiet colors, nothing gets emphasis and the whole screen reads as a toy.

Warm charcoal UI, one terracotta action color, and world palettes that change with time of day.

Characters

Two-head chibi bodies with dot faces

Heads are about 40% of the 28px sprite and faces are a template, so Mira, Wren and Tobi differ mainly by hair color. An 8px face can't show emotion, so it lives in a separate icon.

About three heads tall, silhouette hooks per character, moving brows and mouth, and 48px portraits with six expressions.

Light

Rooms are evenly lit and timeless

The Otherworld room has no light source and no shadow direction. Generated maps like the bookshop are richer but still flat midday. Without light, there is no mood.

A runtime light layer with ambient light plus 2–4 named sources, quantized into hard pixel bands. It also works on the existing generated maps.

Type

Pixel fonts carry every word

Pixelify, Press Start and Silkscreen set the dialogue, body copy and form labels. Long lines are tiring to read, and Chinese falls back to ZCOOL, which looks like a different game.

A literary serif for dialogue and titles, a clean sans for UI, and a mono for labels. Pixels stay inside the world.

Frames

Every element wears the same ornament

Rounded 9-slice candy frames, sparkles, kicker pills, scalloped bars and bobbing icons all sit at the same level, so the eye has nowhere to land. Four equal candy buttons give no first action.

Hairlines and a 3px chamfer. Gold only marks focus and selection, and one primary action per view.

Dialogue

Conversation happens in an empty box

The narrator box beside the stage is mostly blank. Speakers have no portrait, emotion is a tiny head icon, and relationship changes are hidden in the Story log.

Two modes: ambient bubbles for autonomous chatter, and a focused panel with portrait, mood and relationship cues for real conversations.

02 · System

One language from the floorboards to the form fields

Eastward-like craft in the environments, In Stars and Time and Until Then warmth in the faces, and SIGNALIS and NORCO restraint in the interface. These are references for qualities only. The art, palette and characters here are original to PLOT TWIST!.

Light tells the story

Every scene has a time of day and two to four named light sources. Warm key light, cool fill, and shadows lifted toward blue, never black.

Calm world, vivid people

Environments are rich but muted at the edges. Faces, emotion glyphs and character accents are the most saturated things on screen.

Materials, not colors

Wood, brass, plaster, paper and cloth each get a 4–5 step ramp that shifts violet in shadow and amber in light.

Rooms belong to someone

Props say who lives here and what just happened. Detail gathers at the walls and edges, and the floor stays the calmest area.

The interface is paper and slate

Real typography for words, pixels for the world. Hairlines and chamfered corners replace candy frames.

Warm, never grim

Every scene keeps at least one warm light. Shadows stay above #1c1b21. Humor lives in poses and reactions.

03 · Characters

Same three people, a little older

Names, personalities and relationships are unchanged. Each one gets a silhouette hook that still reads at 28 pixels, the same face construction in sprite and portrait, and six expressions shared by every character.

Emotional feedback · glyphs in the world, faces in the panel
Pixel standards
Stage
320 × 192 art px, drawn at whole-number device scale (already true in camera.ts).
Sprite
18 × 36 cell, figure 30–32 px, head 12 px: about 2.7 heads instead of today's 2. Separate arms, torso and legs.
Portrait
48 × 48 bust, shown at 2× or 3×. Same hair, colors and accessories as the sprite.
Outline
1 px, generated from each pixel's own color (darkened and shifted violet). Never black, never thicker when zoomed.
Camera
Three-quarter top-down for everything in a scene, unchanged from the current guide.
Light
Ambient plus named sources, four hard bands each, with a 2×2 dither only at band seams. Sky, flames and glass are emissive. Actors keep 40% of their base brightness so they never vanish into shadow.
Shadow floor
Darks are lifted toward blue-violet. Nothing goes below #1c1b21.
Animation
Breathing (1 px every 1.1 s), blinks, head turns via eye position, a 3-frame emote pop. All stepped, no easing.
Density
Detail at walls and edges, open floor in the middle, 2–4 interactive objects. The ground rules in PIXEL_ART_STYLE_GUIDE.md stay.
Interactive
Gold diamond marker plus a hairline outline on hover. Never a glow or bloom.
04 · Areas

How each part of the game moves

Character art

  • Redraw the 6 bundled sprites and portraits on the new proportions, one character at a time, starting with Mira, Wren and Tobi.
  • Generated sprites get the same spec in /api/ai/sprite prompts and in pixelize.ts: cell size, head ratio, outline rule.
  • Expressions: neutral, smile, angry, worried, surprised, shy, mapped onto the ten emotions the behavior engine already produces.

Dialogue UI

  • Ambient: short ivory bubble over the speaker for autonomous chatter. No portrait.
  • Focused: slate panel with a portrait that rises over the panel's top edge, a mono name with an accent tick, an italic mood, the line in serif, and cues only when data changed.
  • On phones the panel docks under the stage so it never covers characters.

Environments

  • Add a runtime light layer to PixelStage: time of day plus light points stored with the scene. Existing and generated maps gain atmosphere without new images.
  • Map prompts move from "warm cozy" toward named materials, a time of day and storytelling props, with the density rules kept.
  • Each world keeps its own palette: dusk lavender for Otherworld, lamplit amber for Western Fantasy, sodium and neon for Modern, lantern vermilion for Ancient China.

Editor UI

  • Same tokens, but editors read as tools: left navigation, a central canvas or preview, and a right inspector.
  • Long written fields sit on paper surfaces in serif, so a character's story reads like a page.
  • Advanced options go behind disclosure rows. Nothing is removed.
